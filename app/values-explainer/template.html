DDF Value Logic

How each source turns into a value on the trade chart, with the chart engine's own numbers. Read it to decide whether you agree with each step, and use the checks and the list at the bottom to spot what looks wrong.

1 · The map

Four families of series, three views

Every series is measured against one anchor: the ESPN two-tier leg (our model run on ESPN's per-game projections). The views change only the published charts; the other series look the same in all three views.

2 · Checks

Totals and starting points against the anchor

Each total is taken over the players that series and the ESPN anchor both price (QB/RB/WR/TE). A ratio of 1.00 means the series spends the same pie as the anchor. Only the gated rows block a deploy.

Where each position's curve starts (Indexed view)

The top value at each position. Published charts are pinned to fixed starting points; projection series start wherever their own projections put them.

3 · Curves

Value by positional rank

Each line is one series sorted by its own values, so you see its shape, not its player order. Dashed lines mark the anchor's starter and bench cut-offs at this position.

4 · Pie split

How each series divides its value

Share of each series' total that goes to each position, split starter and bench by the ESPN tier. Cells are tinted by the gap to the anchor's share: blue where the series gives less than the anchor, red where it gives more.

6 · Trace a player

One player through every source

The arithmetic for one player, step by step, ending at the value the chart shows in each view.

7 · Compare players

Top players across every series

Ranked by DDF Value. Each cell is tinted against that row's median: red above, blue below. Spread is the highest minus the lowest published chart.

8 · League settings

What moves when the league changes

Top value per position in the Indexed view at other team counts and scorings (standard roster). Published charts are saved once per scoring at 12 teams; every other team count is derived in the browser.
